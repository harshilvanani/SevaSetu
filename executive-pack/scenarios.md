# Real-world scenarios

Three stories that show SevaSetu at work.

- **Numbers:** figures marked *(illustrative)* are realistic estimates for the presentation, not measured results.
- **Status:** steps marked **[Planned]** belong to Phase 2 and are not built yet. Everything else works today.
- **People:** shown by role, not by name.

## Scenario 1 · The December 25–29 Retreat Seva

**Flows used:**
- F1 Apply for a big event **[Planned]**
- O2 Find and invite volunteers
- O5 Build a team
- V2 Find and join a seva
- O4 Complete an event and log hours
- O6 See the network and act on alerts

### The story (slide)
The Retreat Centre coordinator needs **47 volunteers** for five days of full-time seva over the December visit. The 47 cover Kitchen, Housekeeping, Audio-video, Reception, Childcare, Grounds, Shuttle drivers and Office.

**Before SevaSetu**
- A long Google Form collects about 60 applications. Health answers and licence plates sit in a shared spreadsheet anyone with the link can open.
- The coordinator reads every row by hand to match people to departments, then calls and messages each one.
- "Did you get my application?" messages pile up on WhatsApp.
- After the retreat, nobody knows how many hours were given.

**With SevaSetu**
1. **[Planned]** Volunteers apply inside SevaSetu: dates, housing, department experience, signed waiver. Health answers are private and deleted 30 days after the event.
2. The coordinator sees each applicant's **match %** and the reasons, and fills Kitchen first with experienced cooks.
3. The Kitchen and Audio-video **teams** already exist. Team Builder shows the missing skills, such as food safety, and suggests who to invite.
4. **[Planned]** Accepted volunteers are placed in their seva automatically. People not selected are told after the deadline.
5. The dashboard flags departments that are still short, and the coordinator taps straight through to Find volunteers.
6. On December 29 the coordinator completes the event. **Hours and people reached** go into the impact report the same day.

**Result** *(illustrative)*
- About **2 days of coordinator time saved** on matching and follow-up.
- **Every seat filled 10 days earlier.**
- **No health data in shared spreadsheets.**
- **Hours** for all 47 volunteers recorded and ready for the year-end report.

### Business case (speaker notes)
- **Problem:** manual applications are slow, error-prone and expose sensitive data. Coordinators can't see gaps until it's late.
- **Solution:**
  - application form [Planned]
  - explained matching
  - standing teams with skill gaps
  - live staffing alerts
  - one-tap hours logging
- **Measurable result:**
  - time to fill all roles
  - coordinator hours spent
  - share of roles filled by experienced volunteers
  - hours recorded per event
  - zero sensitive data outside the app

## Scenario 2 · A weekly satsang and a Part 1 course

**Flows used:**
- O1 Create and publish an event
- V4 Ask to join a private course
- O2 Find and invite volunteers
- V2 Find and join a seva
- V3 Report progress and earn verified hours
- O3 Review progress

### The story (slide)
A Toronto centre holds a **satsang every Thursday**. Each month it also runs a **Part 1 course**, which needs hall setup, meals, teacher hospitality and outreach calls.

**Before SevaSetu**
- Every week the organizer re-posts the same message asking for setup, prasad and photos.
- The same few people always say yes, and they burn out.
- The course is private, so volunteers ask around to find out about it.
- The organizer can't tell who can be trusted with teacher hospitality.
- Outreach calls are counted on paper, if at all.

**With SevaSetu**
1. The organizer creates the satsang from a **template** and taps **Repeat weekly**. Twelve weeks are published in one step.
2. Volunteers in the area see it on Discover with a **match %** and join in a tap. The last spot can never be double-booked.
3. The Part 1 course is **private**: volunteers see only the name and date, and **ask to join**. The organizer approves.
4. Teacher hospitality **requires Part 1**, so only people who have done the course can take it.
5. Outreach volunteers **report calls as they go** ("15 of 40"). The organizer approves, and the hours appear on each volunteer's profile.
6. **Find volunteers** brings in new people who match, instead of asking the same few again.

**Result** *(illustrative)*
- **One hour a week** of re-posting saved.
- **New volunteers in 3 of every 4 roles** within two months.
- **Outreach progress** visible to everyone.
- **Private course details** shared only with approved people.

### Business case (speaker notes)
- **Problem:** repetitive admin, volunteer burnout, informal access to private courses, untracked work.
- **Solution:**
  - templates and weekly repeat
  - matching that widens the volunteer pool
  - private-event requests
  - course requirements
  - verified progress reports
- **Measurable result:**
  - organizer time per week
  - number of distinct volunteers per month
  - roles filled more than 3 days ahead
  - verified outreach calls

## Scenario 3 · A new centre joins (for example, Halifax)

**Flows used:**
- A1 Approve a new centre
- O1 Create and publish an event
- O5 Build a team
- V1 Sign up and build a profile
- V5 Join a standing team
- O6 See the network and act on alerts
- A3 Run a live demo safely

### The story (slide)
A volunteer teacher in **Halifax** wants to start regular seva. There's no centre on SevaSetu yet, and only a handful of known volunteers.

**Before SevaSetu**
- A new centre starts from zero: a new WhatsApp group, a new spreadsheet, no history.
- National leaders don't see the new centre until someone sends a report.

**With SevaSetu**
1. The teacher **applies to organize**. A platform admin reviews and approves within a day. Every decision is recorded.
2. Before going live, the teacher watches a **live demo** of how other centres run seva, using sample data only.
3. The teacher publishes a first event from a **template**.
4. Local volunteers **sign up**, add their skills and languages, and appear in the centre's **directory** once they serve or choose to be found.
5. The teacher creates an **Outreach team** and a **Hospitality team**. Volunteers accept their invitations.
6. National leaders see **Halifax appear in Atlantic Canada** on the regional coverage view, with its first hours and people reached.

**Result** *(illustrative)*
- **Live within a week of applying.**
- **25 volunteers and 2 teams** in the first two months.
- **Visible to national leadership from the first event**, with no extra reporting.

### Business case (speaker notes)
- **Problem:** new centres start without tools or visibility; growth is hard to see and support.
- **Solution:**
  - approval workflow with an audit trail
  - ready-made templates
  - safe demo for training
  - teams
  - regional dashboards
- **Measurable result:**
  - days from application to first event
  - volunteers and teams after 60 days
  - number of active regions
  - hours per region
